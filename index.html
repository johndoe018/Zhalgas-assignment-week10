How are these example scores interpreted?

The supplied teacher scores are illustrative, holistic judgments. With a strict binary application of the four-point rubric, B explicitly demonstrates both solutions (1 point), C explicitly demonstrates the reference angle (1 point), and D does not name quadrants I and II (0 points). Their displayed teacher scores of 2/4, 2/4, and 1/4 give partial credit for implied or developing reasoning.

A real teacher should document any partial-credit policy before marking. A receives 4/4 under either approach. This distinction is another reason to review AI scores against a clear, consistently applied rubric.

All four responses and both sets of feedback are prewritten fictional examples of an AI-assisted review workflow. No live AI inference or real student data is used.

03 / THE PROCESS, MADE TRANSPARENT

Behind the AI —
Prompt Engineering

Better instructions create a better starting point. These reusable prompts illustrate the design and review process.

PROMPT 01

Quiz Generation

DESIGN THE ASSESSMENT

Act as an experienced mathematics teacher. Create a seven-question assessment about trigonometric equations for secondary school students. Include six multiple-choice questions and one open-ended question. Use the interval 0° ≤ x < 360°. Provide four plausible options per multiple-choice question, identify correct answers, and explain each solution. Ensure all calculations and answers are mathematically accurate.

PROMPT 02

Feedback Generation

SUPPORT THE LEARNER

Act as a supportive mathematics teacher. Evaluate four sample student responses to a trigonometric equation question using a four-point marking rubric. For each response, provide a score out of four, identify strengths, explain mathematical errors or omissions, and suggest a specific next step. Keep the feedback constructive and understandable.

PROMPT 03

Critical Review and Improvement

KEEP HUMAN JUDGMENT CENTRAL

Review the AI-generated feedback for mathematical accuracy, fairness, rubric alignment, clarity, and usefulness. Identify inaccurate scores, overly generic comments, and missed opportunities to acknowledge partial understanding. Revise the feedback to provide specific, constructive teacher-reviewed comments, and explain each modification.

Prompts are shared for transparency and reuse. They are not executed by this website.

04 / LOOKING BACK, THINKING FORWARD

The most important
part is still human.

A personal reflection on using AI responsibly in mathematics assessment.

MZ
Maizhanov ZhalgasStudent ID: 240105093
“Reflection

Creating this assessment platform helped me understand how artificial intelligence can support teachers in designing quizzes and providing feedback. AI was especially useful for generating trigonometric questions, preparing answer explanations, and organizing feedback in a clear format. It saved time and offered ideas that I could improve.

However, AI was not always accurate when evaluating students’ understanding. In one example, it gave full marks to an answer that contained correct solutions but lacked sufficient explanation. Some feedback was also too general and did not provide clear steps for improvement. I reviewed the responses, corrected the scores, and added more specific mathematical guidance.

Next time, I would create a clearer marking rubric before requesting AI feedback and test the assessment with more examples. This experience showed me that AI can assist teachers, but human judgment remains essential for fair assessment.

AI offers a starting point. Thoughtful review makes it better.
